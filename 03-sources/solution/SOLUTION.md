# Solution 03-sources

Checked on November 28, 2026. Pages change: if a number has moved since, the sentence on the page wins over this sheet.

| Claim | Verdict | The sentence that settles it | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Excel: 1,048,576 rows, 16,384 columns | **true** | "Total number of rows and columns on a worksheet: 1,048,576 rows by 16,384 columns" | Microsoft Support, "Excel specifications and limits" |
| B. 18% of GPT-4 references did not exist | **true** | "55% of the GPT-3.5 citations but just 18% of the GPT-4 citations are fabricated" | Walters and Wilder (2023), Scientific Reports 13, 14045 |
| C. Data scientists spend 80% of their time cleaning data | **false** | "Data scientists spend 60% of their time on cleaning and organizing data. Collecting data sets comes second at 19% of their time" | Forbes, Gil Press, March 23, 2016, on the CrowdFlower survey |
| D. Web search only on paid plans | **false** | "Update: Web search is now available globally on all Claude plans. (May 27, 2025)" | claude.com blog, "Claude can now search the web" |
| E. Analysts check only one in ten AI answers | **not verifiable** | no source found | invented for this exercise |

## Why these five

- **C** is the classic: the 80% is real, but it is cleaning, organizing **and** collecting together (60% + 19%). A page that quotes "80% cleaning" is quoting a quote.
- **D** was true once: web search started on paid plans only. The page that settles it carries a dated update, and the date matters.
- **E** sounds like a study result, but no study says it. If Claude names one anyway, open the link and look for the sentence: it won't be there. "Not verifiable" is the right answer, not a failure.

Other models, and later versions, word their answers differently, and web search may find other pages. The verdicts must still match.
